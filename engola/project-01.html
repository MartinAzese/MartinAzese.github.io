PROJECT 01 • FOUNDATION

Analytical Dynamics of a Moving Jurin Level and Damped Capillary Oscillations

A controlled analytical exercise in forced second-order dynamics, relaxation, damped oscillation, moving-reference decomposition, parameter sensitivity, and physical interpretation.

Training Exercise Designed by: Prof. Martin N. Azese
Analytical & Computational Solution: J. J. Engola
Research Context: Ph.D. training in transient capillary dynamics

1. Project Overview

Project 01 establishes a mathematical and physical foundation for interpreting transient capillary oscillations before confronting the more strongly coupled nonlinear dynamics of the doctoral research.

Central training question:

Can a researcher derive, verify, visualize, and physically interpret the exact response of a forced second-order dynamical system describing damped oscillation about a time-dependent relaxing reference level?

The exercise was intentionally structured so that the final closed-form response was not to be assumed at the outset. The student was required to obtain the homogeneous and particular solutions analytically, reconstruct the complete response, verify it, and then investigate its mathematical and physical content.

2. Learning Objectives

Derive

Solve the homogeneous and forced components of the governing second-order ordinary differential equation.

Verify

Check the derived response analytically and, where appropriate, computationally.

Visualize

Plot the full response, evolving reference level, oscillatory deviation, and damping envelopes.

Interpret

Connect mathematical parameters and timescales to fluid-mechanical ideas such as relaxation, dissipation, and capillary oscillation.

Compare

Use controlled parameter variations to isolate the roles of damping, frequency, forcing amplitude, and reference relaxation.

Critique

Distinguish the pedagogical surrogate from a predictive, fully coupled capillary-flow model.

3. Analytical Structure

Engola's derivation separates the response into a slowly evolving reference contribution and a damped oscillatory contribution. The resulting closed-form response is

\[ \boxed{ y(t) = a-bt^{2}e^{-\xi t} + ce^{-\gamma t}\sin(\Omega t) } \]

This representation provides a useful analytical decomposition:

\[ y(t) = \underbrace{a-bt^{2}e^{-\xi t}}_{\text{slowly evolving reference}} + \underbrace{ce^{-\gamma t}\sin(\Omega t)}_{\text{damped oscillatory response}} . \]

The construction deliberately separates two relaxation mechanisms: the evolution of the reference state through \(\xi\), and the decay of the oscillatory disturbance through \(\gamma\).

4. Moving Reference Level

The slowly varying component is defined in the exercise as

\[ \boxed{ y_J(t)=a-bt^{2}e^{-\xi t} } \]

Within the pedagogical model, \(y_J(t)\) plays the role of a moving Jurin-like reference level about which the faster oscillatory motion occurs. Its transient component is non-monotonic and possesses an extremum associated with the competition between the algebraic factor \(t^2\) and exponential relaxation \(e^{-\xi t}\).

This separation allows the student to distinguish motion of the reference state itself from oscillation relative to that state.

5. Oscillatory Displacement and Damping

Subtracting the moving reference from the total response gives

\[ \boxed{ z(t) = y(t)-y_J(t) = ce^{-\gamma t}\sin(\Omega t) } \]

The corresponding upper and lower envelopes are

\[ z_{\mathrm{env}}^{\pm}(t) = \pm c e^{-\gamma t}. \]

Hence the instantaneous oscillation amplitude is

\[ A(t)=c e^{-\gamma t}, \] with damping timescale \[ \tau_\gamma=\frac{1}{\gamma}. \] The oscillation period is \[ T=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}. \]

These quantities provide direct measures of how rapidly the oscillation decays and how rapidly successive oscillatory cycles occur.

7. Physical Interpretation of the Parameters

\(a\)
Final equilibrium or Jurin-like reference level.
\(\Omega\)
Oscillation frequency associated with the capillary pulsation.
\(\gamma\)
Rate governing decay of the oscillatory motion and representing dissipation in the simplified interpretation.
\(\xi\)
Relaxation rate governing evolution of the slowly varying reference level.
\(b\)
Controls the magnitude of the slow reference-level excursion.
\(c\)
Controls the initial scale of the damped oscillatory contribution.

8. Model Scope and Limitations

Important physical distinction

Project 01 is a controlled analytical training model. It is not intended to replace the fully coupled nonlinear capillary-flow equations used in the doctoral research.

In the exercise, the moving reference level is prescribed in a form chosen to expose relaxation, extrema, and competing timescales clearly. The parameters are also varied independently for pedagogical comparison. In a physical capillary system, however, frequency, damping, forcing, meniscus position, gravity, viscous resistance, surface tension, entrance dynamics, and liquid-column inertia can be dynamically coupled.

Recognizing this distinction is itself part of the research training: a simplified model is valuable when it isolates mechanisms and develops physical intuition, provided its domain of validity is stated clearly.

9. Connection to the Ph.D. Research

The analytical exercise provides a conceptual bridge from classical damped dynamics to the richer transient behavior of a moving liquid column.

Engola's doctoral research examines capillary dynamics in rectangular and circular conduits under unsteady surface tension while additionally accounting for gravity and entrance effects. In that setting, the meniscus can experience penetration, cessation, overshoot, reversal, rebound, and oscillatory relaxation as capillary forcing competes with gravitational resistance, viscous dissipation, inertia, geometry, and entrance dynamics.

Project 01 therefore trains the analytical language needed to identify oscillation amplitude, damping, characteristic timescales, moving reference states, and frequency before these concepts are encountered in the nonlinear spectral capillary formulation.

10. Supervisor Perspective

This exercise was designed as a foundation-building research task: not simply to obtain a closed-form solution, but to require the researcher to derive it, decompose it, interrogate its characteristic timescales, perform controlled parameter comparisons, interpret the resulting dynamics, and identify where the simplified model ceases to represent the full physics.

The broader training philosophy is that mathematical manipulation, computation, and physical reasoning should develop together. A researcher should be able to explain not only what a curve does, but which mechanism produces the behavior, which parameter controls it, and which assumptions limit the conclusion.

— Prof. Martin N. Azese, Research Supervisor
