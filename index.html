From Fundamental Fluid Mechanics to Research Independence

This platform presents research, analytical and computational resources, and selected graduate-training projects developed under the supervision of Prof. Martin N. Azese.

The research program integrates theoretical, computational, and progressively experimental approaches to problems in interfacial transport, capillary dynamics, complex fluids, microfluidics, and unsteady aerospace flows.

A central objective of this platform is to make portions of the research-training process publicly accessible: not only final publications, but also carefully designed analytical problems, student-developed solutions, computational investigations, physical interpretations, and the progression from simplified models toward research-level fluid mechanics.

Research Areas

Interfacial & Capillary Transport

Gravity–capillary competition, unsteady surface tension, imbibition, entrance effects, and transient meniscus dynamics.

Complex & Viscoelastic Fluids

Memory effects, velocity slip, pressure-driven transport, porous media, and transient flow.

Spectral & Computational Fluid Mechanics

Sturm–Liouville decomposition, multimode dynamics, reduced-order modeling, asymptotics, and numerical simulation.

Aerospace Fluid Dynamics

Unsteady aerodynamics, wake dynamics, vortex shedding, flow control, and reduced-order descriptions of transient flows.

Ph.D. Research Training

Research independence is developed progressively—from mathematical formulation and analytical derivation to computation, physical interpretation, model criticism, and original research.

PH.D. RESEARCH TRAINING FLOW CESSATION & VISCOELASTIC DYNAMICS

Hollandine Sami Kouaji — Flow Cessation & Viscoelastic Dynamics

Ph.D. Researcher: Hollandine Sami Kouaji
Research Supervisor: Prof. Martin N. Azese

This doctoral research investigates transient pressure-driven flow and flow cessation in Newtonian and viscoelastic liquids, with particular emphasis on material memory, recoil, modal relaxation, spectral dynamics, and the influence of conduit geometry.

Research Training: Analytical verification, independent reproduction of selected research figures, numerical consistency checks, parameter-trend analysis, and development of physical interpretation have formed part of the doctoral research-training process.

Current manuscript: Unsteady Pressure-Driven Flow Cessation in Newtonian and Maxwell Fluids: Memory, Recoil, and Spectral Relaxation
Status: Under peer review.

Upcoming Training Projects: Formal public research-training modules will be developed and documented progressively as the doctoral training program advances.

Training the Next Generation of Researchers

Graduate research in the AZESE Fluid Dynamics Research & Training program emphasizes progressive development toward research independence. Students are trained to move beyond obtaining numerical answers: they are expected to derive, verify, compute, visualize, interpret, question, and communicate the physics underlying their models.

Research opportunities span theoretical and computational fluid mechanics, interfacial and capillary transport, complex fluids, microfluidics, spectral methods, and unsteady aerospace flows.
