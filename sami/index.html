PRIOR PEER-REVIEWED RESEARCH

Ph.D. Research Focus

Sami's doctoral research examines what happens when a pressure-driven flow is subjected to transient forcing and, in particular, when the driving pressure is removed. The resulting cessation process provides a controlled framework for studying the competition among viscous dissipation, fluid inertia, viscoelastic memory, recoil, modal relaxation, and conduit geometry.

The research develops Newtonian and Maxwell-fluid descriptions using cross-sectionally resolved spectral representations. Particular attention is given to the transition from forced flow to post-cessation relaxation and to the manner in which material memory can alter the decay, reversal, recoil, and long-time response of the fluid.
CURRENT DOCTORAL RESEARCH

Research Development Pathway

The doctoral training is organized around a progression from fundamental transient-flow mechanics to independent verification, interpretation, and research-level analysis.

01 • FOUNDATION

Transient Flow Mechanics

Pressure-driven conduit flow, modal dynamics, viscous relaxation, and the Newtonian baseline used to establish the fundamental mechanics of transient transport.
02 • MEMORY

Viscoelastic Response

Extension from Newtonian relaxation to Maxwell-fluid dynamics, constitutive memory, relaxation time, stress persistence, and the physical consequences of stored elastic memory.
03 • CESSATION

Pressure-Stop Dynamics

Removal of the driving pressure exposes post-forcing decay, cessation timescales, recoil, possible flow reversal, and the competition between viscous dissipation and material memory.
04 • VERIFICATION

Research Reproducibility

Independent verification of analytical developments, numerical solutions, parameter trends, and selected research figures, building the ability to test results rather than simply reproduce them.
05 • INTERPRETATION

Spectral Relaxation & Physical Insight

Integration of modal decay, conduit geometry, material memory, and long-time dynamics into physically interpretable mechanisms, strengthening the transition from verification to independent research judgment.

Current Research Manuscript

Unsteady Pressure-Driven Flow Cessation in Newtonian and Maxwell Fluids: Memory, Recoil, and Spectral Relaxation

The current doctoral manuscript develops a theoretical and computational framework for pressure-driven flow cessation in Newtonian and Maxwell-viscoelastic liquids. The work examines the transient response before and after cessation, with emphasis on modal relaxation, viscoelastic memory, recoil and reversal behavior, and the influence of conduit geometry.

Building on the earlier transient-flow foundation, the doctoral study shifts attention from driven flow to the dynamics that emerge when the forcing is removed. This transition exposes a richer relaxation problem in which Newtonian viscous decay provides the baseline, while Maxwell constitutive memory introduces stress persistence, recoil, possible flow reversal, and mode-dependent relaxation. The resulting framework connects conduit geometry, spectral structure, and material memory within a unified description of post-forcing dynamics.

The manuscript is presently undergoing peer review. Accordingly, this research-training page documents the scientific development and training framework without reproducing or publicly releasing material that should remain associated with the journal review process.

Research Verification & Reproducibility Training

An important component of Sami's doctoral development has been learning to treat a research result not merely as an output to be accepted, but as a result that must be independently interrogated, reproduced, and physically understood.

Analytical Verification

Selected analytical developments are independently checked to confirm their mathematical consistency, limiting behavior, and physical meaning.

Independent Figure Reproduction

Selected research figures are independently replotted as a verification exercise, allowing trends, parameter dependence, and numerical consistency to be examined separately from the original calculations.

Numerical Consistency

Computational results are compared against analytical expectations, limiting cases, and independently reproduced calculations where appropriate.

Physical Interpretation

The training emphasizes explaining why a transient occurs: how viscous diffusion, inertia, material memory, geometry, and spectral modes determine the observed response.

Research-Training Principle

The objective is to progress from reproducing a result to explaining it, from explaining it to testing it, and ultimately from testing existing analysis to developing independent research questions and solutions.
